How it works

Like a film camera,
with a naturalist inside.

The app has three stages. Each one shows you less screen than the one before.

  1. 01 · At home

    Load the film

    Choose where you're going: a park, a trail, your garden. An open-weight model (Gemma 3) writes six field quests to suit the season, the time of day and the place.

    “Find a seed, cone or berry still on its plant.”

  2. 02 · Outside

    The screen goes dark

    You see one quest at a time, and the app reads it aloud. There's no preview, feed or map. When you find the thing, tap I found it, take the photo and put the phone away. The next quest is waiting.

    Works with no signal at all.

  3. 03 · Back home

    Develop the roll

    On your computer, Gemma looks at each photo and writes down what it actually sees. Only then does it decide whether you found what you were looking for. Each frame becomes a page in your field journal.

    About two minutes per photo on a laptop. Put the kettle on.

Designed to be put away

Why open-weight AI

A closed API would break
everything this is about.

⌁

Works where the signal doesn't

Trails and parks are where the signal drops. Field mode needs no network, and the darkroom only needs your own machine.

◎

Your walks stay yours

A roll of photos is a time-stamped map of where you go. With an open model, those photos never reach someone else's server. That's how the app is built, not just a policy.

○

Free to run, forever

No API key, no fee per photo, no subscription. A hand-me-down laptop can run the whole darkroom.

✎

Yours to change

Swap in another model with one setting, edit the prompts in a plain file, or fine-tune it on the plants where you live.

FAQ

Good questions.

What exactly do I do?

Open the camera and tap Load film to get six things to find. Go outside. Each time you find one, tap I found it and take a photo. When you're home, open the roll on your computer and tap Develop. You'll get a field journal of what you found.

Why can't I see if I got it right straight away?

That's on purpose. If the app judged every photo on the spot, you'd stand on the trail staring at a loading spinner, which is the screen time we're trying to remove. Like film, you find out later, and the wait is part of the fun.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. After your first visit, the app is saved on your phone, so field mode works in airplane mode. Photos are stored on the device. Developing needs your computer with the model installed, but not the internet.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. Photos are saved in your browser's storage on your own device. When you develop a roll, the photos go only to the AI model running on your own computer. There are no accounts, no analytics and no server.

What do I need on my computer?

Ollama (free) and the gemma3:4b model, about 3 GB. Any modern laptop works and no graphics card is needed; it's just slower on a CPU (about 2 minutes a photo). The quick start takes about five minutes.

I only have a phone. Can I still use it?

Yes. You can load film and go on walks from your phone alone. If no AI is available, the app uses a built-in deck of seasonal quests. Your rolls stay on the phone until you can develop them on a computer, with Export roll on the phone and Import roll on the computer.

What does "glass-free %" mean?

The share of your walk when the app's screen was not showing. If you walk for 40 minutes and look at the quest for 2 minutes in total, you were 95% glass-free. Higher is better, and it's the only number we track.

Can I use a different AI model?

Yes. Any vision model in Ollama works: gemma3:12b on a stronger machine, gemma3n for something lighter, llava, and others. Change it in the app's settings. The prompts are in ollama.js if you want to change how it writes.

Is it good for kids?

It works really well for kids. The quests are short and ask for things that are safe to find, and they tell you to leave nature as you found it. There are no ads, accounts or social features. We'd still suggest an adult goes along.

Is it free and open source?

Yes, it's MIT licensed. The code is on GitHub. It was built for the DEV Hacktoberfest 2026 Open-Source AI Challenge.
